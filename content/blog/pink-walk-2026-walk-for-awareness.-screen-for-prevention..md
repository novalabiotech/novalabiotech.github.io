# What are FIT-Hb and FIT-Tf?

## FIT-Hb: Detecting hidden blood in stool

**FIT-Hb — Fecal Immunochemical Test for Hemoglobin** detects human hemoglobin in stool.

Tiny amounts of blood may be released from colorectal polyps, cancers or other gastrointestinal conditions even when the stool looks completely normal.

FIT-Hb is widely used internationally as a **non-invasive colorectal cancer screening test**.

### Why is FIT-Hb important?

It is:

- **Non-invasive**
- Simple stool-based testing
- No bowel preparation
- No endoscopy for the initial screening
- Specific for human hemoglobin
- More convenient than traditional chemical occult blood testing
- Useful for identifying people who should undergo further evaluation

***

# FIT-Tf: Detecting Transferrin in Stool

**FIT-Tf detects human transferrin**, another blood-associated protein that can be present when gastrointestinal bleeding occurs.

Transferrin can remain comparatively stable in stool and can provide **additional information about occult gastrointestinal bleeding**, particularly where hemoglobin may undergo degradation.

### Why use transferrin?

FIT-Tf may help:

**Detect hidden GI bleeding → identify people requiring further evaluation → support earlier clinical investigation.**

It can complement hemoglobin-based stool testing in selected screening and clinical settings.

> **Important:** FIT-Hb is the established stool-based test used in international colorectal cancer screening programs. FIT-Tf should be considered an additional marker of gastrointestinal bleeding rather than a replacement for diagnostic evaluation.

***

# FIT-Hb + FIT-Tf: Looking for Hidden Bleeding from Two Markers

Using hemoglobin and transferrin markers can provide a broader approach to detecting **occult gastrointestinal bleeding**.

### FIT-Hb

Detects **human hemoglobin**

### FIT-Tf

Detects **human transferrin**

### Together

They can provide additional information when evaluating possible hidden GI bleeding.

A positive result does **not mean that you have cancer**.

It means:

### Hidden blood marker detected

↓

### Clinical evaluation recommended

↓

### Colonoscopy or other investigation if advised by a doctor

# Who Should Consider Colorectal Cancer Screening?

You may particularly benefit from discussing screening if you are:

### ✅ 45 years or older

Many international colorectal cancer screening guidelines recommend beginning routine screening for **average-risk adults from around age 45**.

Screening may also be considered earlier if you have:

- A **family history of colorectal cancer**
- Previous colorectal polyps
- A strong family history of polyps
- Long-standing inflammatory bowel disease
- Other hereditary or clinical risk factors

People with increased risk should discuss the **appropriate screening method and starting age with their doctor**.

***

# Who Should NOT Rely on This Screening Test Alone?

A stool screening test is intended mainly for people who **do not already have significant warning symptoms**.

Do not delay medical evaluation simply to obtain a FIT test if you have:

- 🔴 Visible blood in stool
- 🔴 Black or tarry stool
- 🔴 Persistent unexplained abdominal pain
- 🔴 Unexplained weight loss
- 🔴 Persistent change in bowel habits
- 🔴 Significant unexplained anemia
- 🔴 Known colorectal cancer or a previously identified significant colorectal lesion

These situations may require **direct clinical assessment and possibly colonoscopy or endoscopy**.

For women, stool collection should preferably be avoided during **active menstrual bleeding** because contamination with blood may affect interpretation.

***

# What Happens if My Test is Positive?

### A positive result is NOT a cancer diagnosis.

Blood in stool can occur for several reasons, including:

- Colorectal polyps
- Colorectal cancer
- Hemorrhoids
- Gastrointestinal inflammation
- Ulcers
- Other sources of gastrointestinal bleeding

However, an abnormal screening result should **not be ignored**.

## Positive Test → Clinical Consultation → Further Investigation

Your healthcare professional may recommend:

### Colonoscopy

to examine the colon and identify the source of bleeding.

Depending on the clinical situation, additional gastrointestinal evaluation may also be advised.

***

# What if the Test is Negative?

A negative result means the marker was **not detected in that particular sample**.

It does not provide a lifetime guarantee against colorectal cancer.

Screening works best when it is performed **at the recommended interval**, and people who develop warning symptoms should seek medical evaluation even if a previous stool test was negative.

***

# Why Screening Matters

Colorectal cancer can develop quietly.

The progression can occur over several years:

**Normal colon → Polyp → Precancerous lesion → Colorectal cancer**

Screening creates an opportunity to identify abnormalities **before serious disease becomes clinically obvious**.

That is the real value of preventive screening.
